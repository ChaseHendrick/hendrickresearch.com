Overview

Polytetrafluoroethylene formed into fibers or related textile structures. A membrane is not necessarily a spun fiber.

Feel and behavior

Low-friction technical material.

Where you find it

Filtration, industrial textiles and specialized components.

Source and geography

PTFE fiber is made from polytetrafluoroethylene, a fluoropolymer polymerized from tetrafluoroethylene gas through industrial fluorine chemistry. Fluoropolymer manufacture historically used perfluorinated surfactants such as PFOA as polymerization aids, which later drew regulatory attention to the whole supply chain. Fibrous PTFE products are niche: split-film and monofilament yarns, staple for needle-punched filter felts, sewing threads for filter bags, expanded PTFE membranes and hollow fibers. Many products described as 'PTFE fabric' are laminates of an expanded PTFE membrane on another textile rather than PTFE fiber.

Advantages

  • It resists nearly all chemicals, suiting corrosive filtration and chemical plant textiles.
  • Expanded PTFE membranes are durable and highly waterproof yet vapor-permeable.
  • It tolerates high service temperatures for a polymer and does not support combustion in normal conditions.
  • Its low surface energy helps dust cakes release from filter surfaces.

Drawbacks

  • PTFE is a fluoropolymer that falls under broad PFAS definitions, and its manufacture and disposal draw regulatory scrutiny.
  • Membranes lose breathability when fouled by skin oils and dirt.
  • Fibers are dense, low in strength and prone to creep compared with other technical fibers.
  • It is expensive, hard to dye and hard to bond without special treatments.

Worth knowing

  • The EU's 2023 PFAS restriction proposal drew more than 5,600 scientific and technical comments during its six-month consultation.
  • California's AB 1817 measures PFAS in textiles as total organic fluorine, a screening approach that does not identify individual compounds.
  • A 2017 Leeds study argued that fluorinated repellents in outdoor apparel were 'over-engineering', providing oil repellency beyond what most users need.

Types, grades and fabrics

Types and grades

PTFE reaches textiles in several distinct forms. Expanded PTFE (ePTFE) is a microporous membrane whose pores are small enough to block liquid water but let water vapor pass; it is laminated to fabrics rather than spun. PTFE fiber is made as monofilament, multifilament sewing thread for filter bags, and split-film staple for needled felts. PTFE-coated glass fabric is a glass textile with a fluoropolymer coating, used for architectural roofs and conveyor belts. Rainwear membranes are increasingly made without PTFE: Gore introduced expanded polyethylene (ePE) membranes from Fall/Winter 2022, and other brands use polyurethane or polyester membranes.

Fabrics and products

Waterproof, breathable laminates are built as two-layer (membrane bonded to the face fabric with a loose lining), two-and-a-half-layer (membrane with a printed or coated protective layer) and three-layer (membrane sandwiched between face and backer) constructions for jackets, trousers, gloves and footwear liners. Firefighter turnout gear has used ePTFE moisture barriers. Industrial products include ePTFE membrane laminated onto filter felts for fine-dust capture, all-PTFE felts for corrosive gases, PTFE thread for sewing hot-gas filter bags, and PTFE-coated glass fabric for tensile roofs.

Buying and care

How to judge quality

For rainwear, a membrane is only one layer: check that seams are taped, and that waterproofness and breathability figures state the test method, since numbers from different methods cannot be compared. Ask whether the membrane is ePTFE, ePE, polyurethane or polyester, and whether the durable water repellent is fluorinated. In California, AB 1817 banned intentionally added PFAS in most textile articles from January 1, 2025, with a total organic fluorine threshold of 100 ppm dropping to 50 ppm in 2027; outdoor apparel for severe wet conditions has until 2028 but needs PFAS disclosure. Red flags are 'PFC-free' claims that cover only the finish, not the membrane.

Care in detail

Wash membrane garments when they are dirty, because skin oils and grime reduce breathability and make the face fabric wet out. Close zips and flaps, use a liquid detergent in a warm or cool wash as the label allows, skip fabric softener and powder residues, and rinse thoroughly. If the label permits, tumble drying on a low to medium setting can help refresh the water-repellent finish; otherwise reproof with a wash-in or spray repellent. Store dry and loosely hung. Do not heat PTFE products above their rated temperature or burn them, because decomposition fumes are hazardous.

Care at a glance

Use the manufacturer’s application-specific maintenance and handling instructions. Technical performance cannot be inferred from household laundry guidance.

Environmental and social footprint

PTFE's footprint debate centers on PFAS. In January 2023 five national authorities submitted an EU restriction proposal covering about 10,000 PFAS, and after more than 5,600 consultation comments ECHA published an updated background document on 20 August 2025 that adds derogations, including for some fluoropolymer uses; the restriction was not yet adopted in the sources reviewed. California and New York bans on PFAS in apparel began in 2025. Durable water repellents are a related but separate issue: a Leeds study found non-fluorinated finishes matched fluorinated ones for water repellency though not oil repellency, supporting a shift away from fluorinated finishes in outdoor wear. Gore states its ePE membrane contains no intentionally added PFAS.

Tradeoffs

Evaluate the specified grade, whole product, useful lifetime and realistic recovery route. A technical property does not establish a lower overall impact.

A useful question to ask: What exact material, product form and documented producer stand behind this name?

Structure and chemistry

PTFE's chain is a carbon backbone fully sheathed in fluorine atoms, which twist it into a helix. The strong carbon-fluorine bonds and the fluorine sheath give very low surface energy, low friction, chemical inertness and hydrophobicity. Molar masses reach several million grams per mole, so melt viscosity is far too high for conventional melt spinning. When PTFE paste is extruded and stretched, it develops a microstructure of nodes interconnected by fine fibrils, the basis of expanded PTFE membranes and hollow fibers; dense PTFE lacks these fibrils.

From source to yarn

Because it cannot be melt spun, PTFE fiber is made by indirect routes. In paste extrusion, fine PTFE powder is mixed with a lubricant, extruded, dried, stretched and heated; stretching develops the node-and-fibril structure, and the stretching ratio controls pore size and porosity, with higher ratios raising porosity at the expense of strength. Split-film fiber is made by slitting and fibrillating stretched films, and PTFE split fibers are carded and needle-punched, sometimes with PPS fibers, into filter felts. PTFE hollow-fiber membranes are made by paste extrusion, stretching and heating in the same way.

Performance in use

PTFE withstands almost all chemicals, including strong acids and oxidants, and tolerates high service temperatures, which suits hot corrosive gas filtration and chemical-plant packing. Its low surface energy helps dust cakes release during pulse-jet cleaning, and hydrophobic PTFE hollow fibers kept high filtration efficiency after washing. Weaknesses are low tensile strength, creep and cold flow under load, high density and cost, and poor dyeability. Heated to decomposition, PTFE releases fumes that cause polymer fume fever, a flu-like illness that can progress to lung injury; one reported case involved a plastics machinist who smoked after handling micronized PTFE.

How it is identified

Burn tests are indicative only and should not be attempted: PTFE does not ignite readily, and its decomposition fumes are hazardous. Practical clues are high density, a waxy slippery feel and resistance to almost all solvents. FTIR shows strong carbon-fluorine absorptions, and thermal analysis shows a high crystalline melting transition. Scanning electron microscopy distinguishes expanded PTFE, with nodes linked by fibrils, from dense PTFE or from split fibers with rough, grooved surfaces.

PTFE entered textile engineering through chemical resistance and low-friction applications. Product form, additives and manufacturing history matter more than a broad fluoropolymer label.

The deeper record

PTFE entered textiles through industrial rather than apparel needs: filter bags, threads, packings and membranes for environments too hot or corrosive for other polymers. Expanded PTFE made the polymer porous and breathable, opening filtration, medical and outerwear uses. Its recent history is shaped by PFAS regulation. Studies authored or funded by fluoropolymer producers report that PTFE did not degrade or release non-polymeric PFAS in environmental fate tests and that incineration under best available technology produced no significant amounts of the PFAS studied; an independent review notes that complete breakdown of PTFE requires incineration from about 850 C.

Labeling and law

Fiber content must use the applicable generic name from 16 CFR 303.7 in the US and a fibre name from Annex I of Regulation (EU) No 1007/2011 in the EU; Teflon is a trademark. A garment with a PTFE membrane is labeled by the fibers of its fabric layers. PTFE counts as a PFAS under broad chemical definitions: reviews authored by fluoropolymer producers argue it meets 'polymer of low concern' criteria, while EU restriction proposals have debated covering fluoropolymers as a class.

Frequently asked

Is Gore-Tex PFAS-free?

It depends on the product. The original Gore-Tex membrane is expanded PTFE, a fluoropolymer. Gore introduced an expanded polyethylene (ePE) membrane from Fall/Winter 2022 that the company says has no intentionally added PFAS. Check the product tag or listing for 'ePE' to know which one you have.

Why is my waterproof jacket wetting out?

Usually the durable water repellent on the face fabric has worn off or been fouled, so the outer fabric soaks up water. The membrane may still keep rain out, but a saturated face fabric feels cold and stops vapor escaping, making you damp from sweat. Cleaning and reproofing usually restores beading.

Is PTFE banned?

Not generally, as of the sources reviewed. The EU is still considering a broad PFAS restriction with use-specific derogations for fluoropolymers, and some US states restrict intentionally added PFAS in textiles, with exemptions such as some protective equipment. Rules differ by jurisdiction and product and are changing.

Is Teflon the same as PTFE?

Teflon is a trademark for PTFE and related fluoropolymer products. On a fiber content label, the generic name is used, and a jacket with a PTFE membrane is labeled by the fibers of its fabric layers rather than by the membrane.
