Overview

Production: about 1 million tonnes (clean basis) (global wool production, roughly 0.9% of the world fiber market, 2023). Main producers: Australia, China, New Zealand. [7]

Keratin from sheep fleece. Merino describes a sheep breed and its wool, rather than a fabric construction.

Feel and behavior

Springy, insulating, with a soft hand in finer grades.

Where you find it

Knitwear, tailoring, base layers and blankets.

Source and geography

Wool is the fleece fiber of the domestic sheep (Ovis aries); Merino is one breed line within that species. Merino genetics were long controlled by the Spanish crown, which banned export of the sheep before 1765, after which selected flocks went to Saxony and elsewhere. Merino descended from a Spanish royal flock reached Australia at the end of the eighteenth century, and Australian fine-wool growing later became a large export industry. Today fine apparel wool is traded internationally as greasy fleece, scoured wool and combed top.

Advantages

  • Crimp and resilience let wool garments shed creases after hanging, especially worsted suiting.
  • Wool chars and self-extinguishes rather than melting, which suits upholstery, blankets and workwear.
  • Garments can often be aired between wears instead of washed, which saves laundering energy and wear.
  • The wide micron range lets one fiber serve fine suiting, soft base layers and hard-wearing carpets.
  • It takes acid and wool-reactive dyes deeply, giving rich, even color.

Drawbacks

  • Scales make untreated wool felt and shrink under heat, moisture and agitation.
  • Coarser grades feel prickly because stiff fibers press into the skin.
  • Clothes moth and carpet beetle larvae digest keratin, so stored wool needs protection.
  • Chlorine bleach, strong alkali and protease enzyme detergents damage or dissolve the fiber.
  • Very fine Super numbers and light knits trade durability for softness.

Worth knowing

  • New Zealand's Animal Welfare (Care and Procedures) Regulations 2018 prohibit mulesing by any method, so even a veterinarian using pain relief may not perform it.
  • In one Australian life cycle study, simply changing how emissions were divided between wool and sheep meat changed wool's estimated carbon footprint threefold.
  • Under the IWTO code the word Super may be used only for fabrics of pure new wool, a restriction stated in the code itself.
  • Fresco began as a Hardy Minnis trademark for high-twist tropical wool suiting and is now often used generically for similar open, springy warm-weather cloths.

Types, grades and fabrics

Types and grades

Wool is traded by fiber diameter in microns more than by breed. In Australian Wool Exchange usage, Merino fleece is grouped roughly as ultrafine (under about 16.5 microns), superfine (about 16.5 to 18.5), fine (18.5 to 20.5), medium (20.5 to 23.5) and broad (above about 23.5). Merino supplies most fine apparel wool. Those micron bands describe the fiber itself. Separately, IWTO Super S cloth numbers such as Super 120s or Super 150s are fabric-labelling designations tied to maximum mean fiber diameter in the finished cloth under the IWTO Fabric Labelling Code of Practice; they are not the same thing as a farm classer's superfine lot ticket. Corriedale is a dual-purpose meat and wool breed with medium, well-crimped fleece that suits knitting yarn and felting. Shetland wool comes from a small native breed of the Shetland Islands and is valued for its many natural shades. Lambswool is wool from a young sheep's first shearing, usually softer, with fine natural tips.

Fabrics and products

Worsted cloth uses combed, parallel fiber spun into smooth, compact yarn. Gabardine is a tight, steep warp-faced twill for suits, trousers and trench coats; wool crepe uses high-twist yarns for a dry, grainy drape in dresses; tropical wool includes open plain weaves and fresco-type high-twist open suitings for warm weather. Fresco, historically a Hardy Minnis trademark and now often used loosely, points to that open high-twist tropical family rather than to a micron grade. Woollen cloth uses carded fiber for lofty, hairy yarn: flannel is typically milled and brushed for a soft face, and tweed is a rugged, often multicolored woollen twill or herringbone for jackets. Wool jersey is a single-knit fabric for tops and dresses, and merino rib and interlock knits dominate base layers. Melton and loden are heavily milled coatings, while felt is matted fiber without weaving. Blends add nylon to socks for abrasion resistance and elastane or polyester to suiting for stretch and crease recovery.

Buying and care

How to judge quality

For anything worn against skin, micron matters most. Prickle comes mainly from the share of stiff fibers coarser than about 30 microns, so fine lots with few coarse fibers feel smooth while cheap, coarse knits can itch. Stretch a knit gently: it should spring back, and a dense, even stitch pills less than a loose, fuzzy one. In suiting, a higher Super S cloth number means finer mean fiber diameter under the IWTO code, not a stronger cloth and not automatically a farm grade called superfine; very fine, light cloths trade abrasion resistance for hand. Ask whether a Super claim refers to tested cloth diameter limits rather than to a marketing adjective. US law requires recycled wool to be disclosed as such, so read the fiber line for recycled wool and exact blend percentages. Superwash or machine washable indicates a shrink-resist treatment. A Woolmark or RWS logo should apply to the specific product, not just the brand.

Care in detail

The care label governs, but general practice is gentle. Hand wash, or use a machine wool cycle only if the label allows, in cool to lukewarm water (about 30 C or below) with a pH-neutral wool detergent that contains no protease enzymes or chlorine bleach. Do not rub, wring or soak in hot water; roll knits in a towel and dry them flat away from direct heat, since tumble drying felts untreated wool. Press with steam at the two-dot setting (ISO 3758 caps it at 150 C), using a damp pressing cloth to avoid shine on worsted. Brush coats and air garments between wears. Blot stains with cool water, because heat sets protein stains. Store wool clean in sealed bags, check stored pieces for moth larvae, and remove pills with a comb or fabric shaver.

Care at a glance

Follow the label. Air between wears; use a wool cycle only if permitted, avoid agitation and dry knitwear flat.

Environmental and social footprint

Wool's footprint is dominated by the farm. A 2016 life cycle study by Wiedemann and colleagues of three Australian Merino regions reported fossil energy of about 12.5 to 22.5 MJ and fresh water of about 204 to 394 liters per kilogram of greasy wool,; how impacts were split between wool and sheep meat changed greenhouse gas estimates threefold. The IWTO argued in 2019 that the Higg Materials Sustainability Index should cover full life cycles rather than stopping at fabric, and in June 2022 Norway's Consumer Authority warned H&M that marketing built on Higg MSI data could mislead. Mulesing has been banned in New Zealand since October 2018 but remains legal in Australia, where states regulate welfare. In a 2023 laboratory study, natural microfibers including wool disturbed oyster digestive and immune functions.

Tradeoffs

Farming, land management, processing energy and garment longevity all matter. A natural origin alone is not an impact assessment.

A useful question to ask: Can the seller identify the wool origin, mill, fiber grade and scope of any animal-welfare certification?

Protein-based natural material

Most hair is keratin; silk is a different protein-fiber system.

Structure and chemistry

Wool is a keratin composite. An outer cuticle of overlapping scales, arranged like roof shingles, surrounds a cortex of spindle-shaped cortical cells held together by a lipid and protein cell membrane complex. Inside each cortical cell, alpha-helical keratin intermediate filaments about 7.5 nm across are embedded in a matrix of keratin-associated proteins, some rich in cystine (high-sulfur) and some rich in glycine and tyrosine. Oxidized cystine forms disulfide cross-links that stabilize the matrix. In fine Merino the cortex is bilateral: orthocortex and paracortex sit side by side, the paracortex carrying more cystine, and the orthocortex lies on the outer side of each crimp wave, which is the structural basis of crimp.

From source to yarn

Sheep are shorn, and fleeces are skirted to remove stained edges and classed by fineness, length and color. Scouring in warm detergent baths removes wool grease (the source of lanolin), suint salts and dirt. Carding opens the fiber into a web. On the worsted route the sliver is gilled and combed to remove short fiber (noil), producing parallel top for smooth, compact yarn; on the woollen route carded fiber is condensed and spun directly for bulky, lofty yarn. Machine-washable (superwash) wool is commonly produced by the chlorine-Hercosett route, in which chlorination degrades scale edges and a polyamide-epichlorohydrin resin then coats the fiber.

Performance in use

Wool is hygroscopic, with moisture regain commonly given at roughly 14 to 18% under standard conditions (65% RH, 20 C), and it releases heat of sorption as it absorbs water vapor. It has a limiting oxygen index of about 25 and an ignition temperature of about 570 to 600 C in Woolmark and CSIRO data, and it chars rather than melting or dripping. The directional frictional effect of the scales lets fibers ratchet toward their root ends under heat, moisture and agitation, which causes felting shrinkage. As an amphoteric protein, wool takes acid, metal-complex and wool-reactive dyes. Keratin-digesting clothes moth and carpet beetle larvae are its main pests.

How it is identified

Burn tests are indicative only and should be done with care: wool usually burns slowly, smells like burning hair, tends to self-extinguish and leaves a brittle, crushable black residue. Under a light microscope, the cuticle scale pattern is diagnostic of an animal fiber. Scanning electron microscopy uses scale height at the scale edge as an objective criterion to separate sheep wool from specialty fibers such as cashmere, and DNA and proteomic methods are used when fiber blends or heavily processed fabrics are in dispute.

History

Timeline

  1. before 1765Spain banned the export of merino sheep, and the crown tightly controlled access to the breed. [4]
  2. 1765A selected royal consignment of merinos went to Saxony, where breeders developed extremely fine wool. [4]
  3. 1797Merinos descended from a flock that Carlos III of Spain had given to the House of Orange reached New South Wales. [4]
  4. 1939The US Wool Products Labeling Act was enacted, defining wool and requiring fiber content disclosure. [5]
  5. 2018New Zealand's Animal Welfare (Care and Procedures) Regulations 2018 prohibited mulesing, with the ban in force from October 2018. [6]

Sheep fleece moved from household spinning and regional cloth trades into mechanized woollen and worsted industries. Merino became associated with fine apparel wool, while Australia became an important growing region. Modern wool spans tailoring, performance layers and interior textiles.

The deeper record

Spain's merino flocks underpinned a fine-wool monopoly until the crown began releasing animals in the later eighteenth century; Saxony received a royal consignment in 1765 and Saxon breeders turned the Merino toward extremely fine wool. Merino descended from a flock that King Carlos III had given to the House of Orange reached New South Wales in 1797, and John Macarthur, aided by large land grants, built flocks whose fine wool rivaled Spanish and Saxon supply. By the late nineteenth century wool was Australia's leading export. In recent decades mulesing, a surgical procedure against flystrike, has become a central welfare controversy that shaped certification schemes.

Labeling and law

In the US, the Wool Products Labeling Act of 1939 (15 U.S.C. 68 et seq.) and the FTC rules at 16 CFR Part 300 govern wool labeling. The statute defines wool as fiber from the fleece of sheep or lamb or hair of the Angora or Cashmere goat, optionally including the specialty fibers of camel, alpaca, llama and vicuna, never reclaimed from a woven or felted product. Under EU Regulation 1007/2011, 'fleece wool' or 'virgin wool' is reserved for wool never previously incorporated in a finished product. The IWTO Fabric Labelling Code of Practice governs 'Super S' terms, and the Textile Exchange Responsible Wool Standard prohibits mulesing on certified farms.

This describes what rules and standards cover. It is not legal advice; jurisdiction, product form and current rule text control.

Frequently asked

Is merino wool itchy?

Itch, or prickle, comes mostly from stiff fibers coarser than about 30 microns pressing on the skin, not from wool as such. Fine merino with few coarse fibers is comfortable for most people, while cheap, coarse knits can prickle. Testing a garment against the neck or inner arm before buying is a practical check.

What does Super 120s mean on a suit?

It means the wool in the cloth has a mean fiber diameter no greater than about 17.75 microns under the IWTO Fabric Labelling Code of Practice, which US wool labeling law also follows for Super claims. It is a cloth-fineness designation, not the same as a farm classer's superfine micron band, and it says nothing about weave, weight, finishing or durability; higher numbers are generally more delicate.

What is the difference between worsted and woollen?

Worsted yarn is spun from combed top with short fibers removed, so it is smooth, compact and strong, as in gabardine, crepe and most suiting. Woollen yarn is spun from carded fiber lying in random directions, so it is lofty, hairy and warm, as in flannel, tweed and blankets. The terms describe processing routes, not breeds.

Why did my wool sweater shrink?

Wool shrinks mainly by felting: with heat, moisture and agitation, the surface scales let fibers ratchet in one direction and lock together. The change is largely irreversible. Superwash-treated wool resists it, but untreated knits should be washed cool, gently and briefly, then dried flat rather than tumble dried.

What is fresco wool cloth?

Fresco usually means an open, high-twist tropical suiting, often worsted wool, with a dry porous hand for warm weather. Hardy Minnis historically trademarked Fresco; many mills now use the word loosely for similar cloths. It describes construction and yarn twist, not a Super S number or a sheep breed.
