Plant

  • Abacá

    Leaf-sheath fiber from Musa textilis, a relative of edible bananas.

  • Bamboo bast

    Mechanically separated bamboo fiber, distinct from viscose made using bamboo-derived pulp..

  • Banana fiber

    Fiber extracted from banana pseudostems or leaf sheaths.

  • Barkcloth

    A sheet formed from prepared inner bark, usually by beating and joining rather than conventional spinning and weaving..

  • Coir

    Fiber separated from the husk of the coconut.

  • Cotton

    A seed fiber with a world of possibilities.

  • Hemp

    A strong stem fiber, made wearable.

  • Henequen

    A hard leaf fiber from Agave fourcroydes, related to but distinct from sisal..

  • Jute

    The golden fiber of working textiles.

  • Kapok

    Light seed floss from the kapok tree, commonly Ceiba pentandra.

  • Kenaf

    Bast fiber from Hibiscus cannabinus, with a woody core that can have separate uses..

  • Linen

    A field of flax, woven into summer.

  • Lotus fiber

    Fine strands drawn from lotus stems and joined for specialty yarn.

  • Milkweed floss

    Seed floss from milkweed plants.

  • Nettle

    Bast fiber from nettle species.

  • Paper yarn

    Narrow paper strips twisted into yarn.

  • Pineapple leaf fiber

    Leaf fiber from the pineapple plant.

  • Raffia

    Strips and fibers from raffia palm leaves.

  • Ramie

    An old bast fiber with a bright surface.

  • Sisal

    A hard leaf fiber from Agave sisalana.

Animal

  • Alpaca

    The Andes, carried in a fleece.

  • Angora

    Rabbit down with a distinctive halo.

  • Bison down

    Fine undercoat recovered from bison and separated from coarse hair..

  • Camel hair

    Warmth gathered from a seasonal coat.

  • Cashmere

    A winter undercoat, a long spring journey.

  • Chiengora

    A term for yarn spun from dog undercoat, usually in small-scale craft contexts..

  • Down

    Soft branching plumage clusters, commonly from ducks or geese.

  • Eri silk

    Silk associated with eri silkworms, commonly prepared as spun staple rather than a continuous reeled filament..

  • Feather

    Bird plumage with a more defined shaft than down.

  • Guanaco

    A Patagonian fiber with a conservation story.

  • Horsehair

    Coarse mane or tail hair used for resilience and structure rather than softness..

  • Llama

    Fleece from Lama glama, with fine and coarse fractions that may need separation..

  • Merino wool

    From open pasture to considered cloth.

  • Mohair

    Luster from the Angora goat.

  • Muga silk

    A silk associated with Assam and the muga silkworm.

  • Possum fiber

    Underfur used in specialty blends, particularly with wool.

  • Qiviut

    An Arctic undercoat, patiently gathered.

  • Rabbit hair

    Rabbit hair from breeds other than, or not identified as, Angora.

  • Silk

    A continuous filament, a luminous cloth.

  • Tussar silk

    A group of non-mulberry silks, with species and geography affecting the finished fiber..

  • Vicuña

    A rare Andean down with a complex provenance.

  • Yak down

    A fine underlayer from the high plateau.

Cellulosic

  • Acetate

    A cellulose derivative, usually cellulose diacetate, spun into fiber.

  • Cupro

    Regenerated cellulose made by a cuprammonium process, often using cotton linters as feedstock..

  • Lyocell

    Cellulose transformed through a solvent process.

  • Modal

    Regenerated cellulose with wet strength in mind.

  • Triacetate

    A more highly acetylated cellulose derivative than ordinary acetate..

  • Viscose

    Plant cellulose, dissolved and remade.

Synthetic

  • Acrylic

    A spun staple with a wool-like hand.

  • Elastane

    A little stretch changes the whole garment.

  • Modacrylic

    A modified acrylonitrile-based fiber category, chemically distinct from ordinary acrylic..

  • Nylon

    Strength designed into a filament.

  • Polyester

    Engineered filaments, everywhere around us.

  • Polypropylene

    An olefin polymer melt-spun into fibers or tapes..

  • PVA / vinal

    Polyvinyl alcohol-based fibers with properties controlled by polymer treatment and processing.

  • PVC / vinyon

    Chlorinated vinyl-polymer fibers.

Recycled

  • Recycled cotton

    Cotton recovered from production scraps or used textiles and reopened for another textile cycle..

  • Recycled nylon

    Polyamide recovered through a recycling route from a specified waste stream.

  • Recycled polyester

    A new filament from an existing polymer.

  • Recycled wool

    Another life for an existing thread.

Technical

  • Carbon fiber

    Carbon-rich filaments produced from a precursor such as PAN or pitch and usually used in a composite system..

  • Meta-aramid

    An aromatic polyamide with a different molecular arrangement from para-aramid, used for thermal performance..

  • Para-aramid

    An aromatic polyamide engineered for high tensile performance.

  • PBI fiber

    Polybenzimidazole, an engineered high-temperature fiber commonly used within protective textile systems..

  • Polyethylene / UHMWPE

    Polyethylene fibers include high-performance ultra-high-molecular-weight grades.

  • PPS fiber

    Polyphenylene sulfide engineered for demanding industrial environments..

  • PTFE fiber

    Polytetrafluoroethylene formed into fibers or related textile structures.

Mineral & metal

  • Asbestos (historical)

    A group of naturally fibrous silicate minerals historically used in technical textiles.

  • Basalt fiber

    Continuous or discontinuous mineral fiber formed from processed basalt rock..

  • Ceramic fiber

    A broad group of inorganic technical fibers, with composition and respirability varying widely..

  • Glass fiber

    Drawn inorganic glass filaments, available in multiple compositions and product forms..

  • Metal fiber

    Fine metallic filaments, shaved fibers or metal-containing yarn structures.

Bio-based

  • Alginate

    Fiber formed from alginic-acid derivatives associated with brown seaweed.

  • PLA

    Polylactic acid, a manufactured polymer that can use fermentation-derived feedstocks.

Regenerated protein

  • Azlon / protein fiber

    Manufactured fiber formed from regenerated protein, historically including casein, soy and other sources..

Specialty

  • Cervelt

    A proprietary name in the rare-fiber archive.

  • Fox fur

    A pelt material with a pronounced pile.

  • Mink

    An archival view of a pelt-based material.

  • Raccoon dog fur

    A species name matters as much as a trade name.

  • Yangir

    A rare trade name that needs careful evidence.
