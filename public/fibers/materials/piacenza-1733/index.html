Source and geography

Piacenza 1733 (Fratelli Piacenza) is a historic Biella-area wool mill house based in Pollone, Italy. The producer publicly dates its founding to 1733. It is not a fiber species. Wool still comes from sheep raised in wool-growing regions worldwide; the mill house name marks processing and design heritage in the Biella district. This atlas entry teaches mill-house provenance. It adds no map supplier edges.

Types, grades and fabrics

Types and grades

Keep distinct: generic wool (and other noble fibers the mill may publish), cloth constructions for apparel and interiors, and the house trademark with its Pollone / Biella place story. Founding-year marketing is heritage narrative, not a lab measurement.

Compare weight, weave, yarn count and finish across cloths when studying modern products from a long-lived mill.

Buying and care

How to judge quality

Look for fiber content, cloth weight, yarn quality and finishing notes on tickets and hangtags. A 1733 founding claim on producer story pages is heritage narrative, not a laboratory quality metric. Prefer even yarn, clean selvedges, secure seams and documented care instructions over antiquity adjectives alone.

Care in detail

Defer to the product care label for washing, drying and ironing limits. Wool cloths often prefer airing, gentle brushing and professional pressing rather than frequent machine washing. Knitwear from wool mills usually needs a gentle cycle or hand wash and flat drying; store pieces clean to discourage keratin pests.

Care at a glance

Follow the finished product’s care label. A trademark does not specify the care needs of every blend or construction.

Environmental and social footprint

Wool mill impacts include water and energy in scouring and finishing and chemical management in dyehouses. Family-mill sustainability claims should be read on current producer pages with dates and clear scope. Longevity of garments and repair culture often matter as much as the mill’s founding century when judging practical footprint.

Tradeoffs

Producer claims apply to defined products and comparisons. This atlas does not independently certify performance, sourcing or environmental claims.

Science

Protein-based natural material

Most hair is keratin; silk is a different protein-fiber system.

Structure and chemistry

Mill-house branding does not alter keratin structure. Merino and other wools remain diameter-graded sheep fibers; blends follow each component. Historic mill collections may emphasize noble fibers and seasonal cloth weights. Always separate the mill trademark from the generic fiber name on the label.

From source to yarn

A vertical or semi-vertical wool mill may scour, card or comb, spin, weave and finish under one house identity, or coordinate stages with partners. Biella district clustering historically supported that chain. Producer sustainability and heritage pages describe current practice; treat them as attributed claims. Farm-to-mill logistics are not inferred by this atlas.

Performance in use

Cloth performance follows yarn count, weave, weight and finish. A three-century founding date does not guarantee a particular hand or durability on every modern SKU. Compare GSM, weave and care instructions across products. Marketing about authenticity should be read beside measurable cloth specs.

How it is identified

No microscopy test identifies Piacenza 1733. Labels, selvedges and swing tickets identify the mill house. Country-of-origin marking may show Italy for weaving or make-up while fiber origin differs. Ask for the fiber content and any mill ticket codes when studying a cloth.

Labeling and law

Wool labeling rules still govern fiber names and percentages. The mill-house trademark is additional commercial information. EU and US fiber regulations do not replace care labels. Heritage dates on marketing sites are not legal fiber designations.

Frequently asked

Is Piacenza 1733 a fiber?

No. It is a historic Biella mill-house trademark. Wool or other fibers named on the label remain the generic materials, separate from the house identity.

Does 1733 mean the wool is Italian-grown?

No. The year is a producer founding story for the mill house. Fiber origin is a separate question from where cloth was spun, woven or finished.

Where does the producer place the mill?

Public producer pages locate Fratelli Piacenza / Piacenza 1733 in Pollone in the Biella textile district of Piedmont, Italy, as the house geography.

Will the atlas draw a route to Piacenza?

No. Reference mill-house brands do not receive invented map edges. Heritage narratives stay in prose and citations, not in globe logistics.
