Batik

Wax protects selected cloth areas from dye to create a patterned textile.

Also searched as: wax-resist dyeing

Open the interactive guide ↗

How it is made

Artisans apply the wax design, dye the cloth and remove the resist. Repeated stages allow additional colors and detail.

How to recognize it

Look for reserved lines and areas, sometimes with fine dye veins where wax cracked.

Common uses

  • Garments
  • Scarves
  • Decorative cloth

What to distinguish

Batik is a process with important cultural traditions, especially in Indonesia, not one flower or geometric motif. A batik-style print need not use wax resist.
