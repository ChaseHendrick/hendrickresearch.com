Intarsia knitting

Intarsia forms separate color areas without carrying each unused yarn across the whole reverse.

Also searched as: Intarsia

Open the interactive guide ↗

How it is made

Each color area has its own yarn supply. Neighboring areas connect where the yarns change, forming the image within the knitted fabric.

How to recognize it

Large color blocks have local joins at their boundaries rather than long backing floats.

Common uses

  • Picture sweaters
  • Color-block knitwear
  • Decorative knit panels

What to distinguish

Stranded colorwork carries unused colors behind the active stitches; intarsia treats the color areas separately.
