Weave structure

Satin

Long floats and separated binding points produce a smooth woven face.

Also searched as: Satin weave

Open the interactive guide ↗

How it is made

The binding points are distributed so neighboring points do not create the continuous diagonal of a simple twill. This example is a five-end warp-faced satin, with one binding point per row and column.

How to recognize it

Look for extended smooth yarn surfaces and a face that catches light differently from the back.

Common uses

  • Eveningwear
  • Linings
  • Furnishings

What to distinguish

Satin names a weave as well as fabrics made with it; it does not by itself mean silk.
