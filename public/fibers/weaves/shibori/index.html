Shibori

Japanese shaped-resist techniques pattern cloth by controlling which areas take dye.

Also searched as: shaped resist dyeing, kanoko, itajime, kumo

Open the interactive guide ↗

How it is made

Binding, stitching, folding or clamping protects portions of the fabric. Different manipulations create dots, grids, lines or ring-like forms.

How to recognize it

Look for repeat relationships that reflect how the cloth was compressed or folded.

Common uses

  • Kimono
  • Scarves
  • Decorative textiles

What to distinguish

Shibori includes more than tie-dye, and blue-and-white color alone cannot identify it. A printed shibori effect does not establish the technique.
