Velvet

An extra warp system forms a dense pile above the woven ground.

Also searched as: Warp-pile velvet

Open the interactive guide ↗

How it is made

Pile warp yarns rise from a ground cloth to form loops that may be cut, left intact or combined in patterns. The short upright pile gives the surface its characteristic light response.

How to recognize it

Gently change the pile direction to see the shade change; examine the backing for a woven ground.

Common uses

  • Eveningwear
  • Upholstery
  • Draperies

What to distinguish

Woven velvet uses a pile warp, while corduroy forms its characteristic pile from filling floats. A velvet-like appearance alone does not identify the fiber.
