Color pattern

  • ArgyleColored diamonds combine with crossing diagonal lines to make a layered geometric repeat.
  • Awning stripeBroad, regular bands create a bold alternating stripe.
  • Bengal stripeAlternating light and colored stripes of approximately equal width.
  • Breton stripeHorizontal stripes associated with the French marinière and nautical knitwear.
  • Buffalo checkA bold, large check commonly associated with alternating red and black blocks.
  • Candy stripeA fine repeated stripe usually made with light and colored bands.
  • Chalk stripeA suiting stripe with a broader, softer-edged appearance than a typical pinstripe.
  • GinghamAn even check formed by overlapping equal-width light and colored bands.
  • Glen checkA compound check that combines smaller checks and striped areas into a larger repeat.
  • Gun club checkA multicolored small check closely related to houndstooth.
  • HoundstoothA broken check whose pointed corners come from color order interacting with twill.
  • Madras checkA colorful checked cotton cloth tradition associated with Chennai, formerly Madras.
  • Pencil stripeA narrow, clearly drawn stripe that resembles a fine pencil line.
  • PinstripeFine parallel lines repeat across a contrasting ground.
  • Regimental stripeDiagonal bands are arranged in repeated color groups, especially on neckties.
  • Tartan and plaidCrossing bands repeat in a defined color sequence called a sett.
  • TattersallA light-ground check made with alternating fine colored lines.
  • Ticking stripeA repeated narrow stripe associated with traditional mattress ticking.
  • WindowpaneWidely spaced crossing lines frame open squares or rectangles.

Knit structure

  • Cable knitCable knitting crosses groups of stitches to make raised ropes, braids or traveling lines.
  • Fair Isle knittingFair Isle is a Shetland colorwork tradition using stranded yarns and repeating knitted motifs.
  • French terryFrench terry pairs a jersey face with an unbrushed looped back.
  • Garter stitchGarter stitch makes a reversible knit with pronounced horizontal ridges.
  • Intarsia knittingIntarsia forms separate color areas without carrying each unused yarn across the whole reverse.
  • InterlockInterlock is a double knit with a smooth, similar appearance on both sides.
  • Jacquard knitJacquard knitting uses selected stitches and yarns to build pattern into a knit.
  • Piqué knitPiqué knit combines knit and tuck stitches to produce a small cellular surface.
  • PointellePointelle uses small deliberate openings to pattern a knitted surface.
  • Ponte di RomaPonte is a firm double knit with a subtle horizontal rib.
  • RaschelRaschel is a versatile warp-knitting family used for meshes, lace and other textured fabrics.
  • Rib knitRib knit alternates face and reverse stitch columns to form expandable lengthwise ribs.
  • Seed stitchSeed stitch alternates knit and purl stitches into a small, pebbled texture.
  • Single jerseySingle jersey is a plain knit with visibly different front and back surfaces.
  • TricotTricot is a warp-knit family often made as fine, relatively flat fabric.

Motif

  • ArabesqueInterlacing curved stems and stylized plant forms create an ornamental network.
  • BotanicalPlants, leaves, branches and other botanical forms provide the subject of a repeat.
  • CamouflageContrasting patches, marks or pixels disrupt a recognizable outline.
  • ChevronContinuous angled bands meet in repeated V-shaped points.
  • Ditsy floralSmall, closely repeated flowers make an all-over pattern.
  • FloralFlowers organize a design ranging from scattered sprigs to dense trailing repeats.
  • Foulard motifSmall, orderly motifs repeat in a regular spaced layout.
  • Greek keyA continuous angular line turns repeatedly at right angles.
  • HarlequinA repeating field of elongated diamonds arranged edge to edge.
  • Leopard motifIrregular rosette-like spots evoke a leopard’s coat.
  • MedallionA self-contained ornamental unit repeats as a rounded, oval or shaped centerpiece.
  • OgeeOpposing S-shaped curves meet to form an undulating pointed repeat.
  • PaisleyCurved teardrop forms with bent tips build an ornamental repeat.
  • Pin dotTiny repeated dots create a subtle spotted surface.
  • Polka dotRegularly repeated round spots contrast with a surrounding ground.
  • QuatrefoilFour rounded lobes surround a center to make a clover-like outline.
  • Toile de JouyDetailed scenic vignettes repeat across a textile ground.
  • TrellisA connected lattice divides the surface into repeated open cells.
  • Zebra motifIrregular branching stripes evoke the contrasting bands on a zebra’s coat.

Surface & finish

  • Bouclé textureBouclé texture comes from a fancy yarn with loops or curls along its length.
  • Brushed surfaceBrushing raises surface fibers to change a fabric's appearance and feel.
  • Burnout / devoréBurnout creates sheer patterns by selectively removing one fiber component from a fabric.
  • Embossed textureEmbossing presses a relief pattern into an existing fabric surface.
  • Flocked surfaceFlocking attaches short fibers to a surface to create a soft, raised design.
  • Moiré finishMoiré describes a rippling, watered appearance on a fabric surface.
  • Slub textureSlub texture uses thicker sections along a yarn to create an uneven surface.
  • Sueded finishSueding gives fabric a fine, suede-like surface through controlled abrasion.
  • Sweatshirt fleeceSweatshirt fleece has a smooth knit face and a raised, fuzzy backing.

Textile technique

  • BatikWax protects selected cloth areas from dye to create a patterned textile.
  • Bobbin laceBobbin lace creates openwork by crossing and twisting multiple threads.
  • CrochetCrochet forms fabric by drawing yarn through loops with a hook.
  • Dobby weavingA shaft-control system produces small repeating woven figures and textures.
  • Dotted SwissA light cloth decorated with small spaced dots, often visibly raised.
  • EmbroideryEmbroidery creates decoration through stitches worked on or through a supporting material.
  • IkatA resist-dyeing method patterns yarn before it is woven into cloth.
  • Jacquard weavingIndividual warp control allows elaborate woven designs with large or detailed repeats.
  • MacraméMacramé builds decorative textiles through arrangements of knots.
  • Needle laceNeedle lace builds an openwork fabric using a needle and thread.
  • Pleated texturePleating folds an existing fabric into a repeated three-dimensional shape.
  • QuiltingQuilting joins fabric layers with lines of stitching that can also form a surface design.
  • ShiboriJapanese shaped-resist techniques pattern cloth by controlling which areas take dye.
  • SmockingSmocking gathers cloth into small folds held and decorated by stitches.
  • Tapestry weavingDiscontinuous colored wefts build a design while covering the warp.
  • Tie-dyeBound or gathered cloth resists dye in selected areas to create a pattern.

Weave structure

  • 2/1 twillA three-thread twill repeat places more warp than weft on the illustrated face.
  • 2/2 twillA balanced four-thread twill shows equal warp and weft interlacing counts.
  • 3/1 twillA four-thread twill puts three warp crossings on the face for every one below.
  • Basket weaveGroups of yarns act together to make a larger version of plain weave.
  • Birdseye weaveSmall repeated eye-like figures give several woven constructions their birdseye name.
  • Broken twillA family of twills interrupts the continuous diagonal through a change in sequence or direction.
  • Crepe weaveIrregularly arranged interlacings create a granular surface without an obvious directional line.
  • Diamond twillTwill directions reverse along both axes to outline woven diamonds.
  • Double clothTwo layers are woven together and may be joined or exchanged to form a design.
  • HerringboneAlternating bands of opposing twill direction create a broken zigzag.
  • Leno weavePaired warp yarns cross around one another to secure an open woven structure.
  • Mock lenoGrouped interlacings produce open areas that resemble leno without crossing warp pairs.
  • Plain weaveEach yarn alternates over and under its neighbors in the smallest woven repeat.
  • Rib weaveAn extended plain-weave structure emphasizes ribs in one direction.
  • SatinLong floats and separated binding points produce a smooth woven face.
  • TwillStaggered yarn floats create the diagonal lines shared by a large family of weaves.
  • Waffle weaveA cellular woven texture forms recessed centers bounded by raised ridges.

Woven fabric

  • BrocadeAdditional pattern yarns create figured decoration over a woven ground.
  • CanvasA firm, typically heavy plain-woven fabric emphasizes sturdy yarns and close construction.
  • ChambrayA colored warp and a lighter filling create a softly mixed color in a plain weave.
  • CorduroyRows of cut pile make corduroy’s lengthwise ribs, known as wales.
  • DamaskContrasting bindings create a figured woven design that reverses between the two faces.
  • DenimA twill fabric commonly combines colored warp yarns with pale filling yarns.
  • DrillA firm twill fabric is widely used where clothing needs substantial body.
  • GabardineA closely set twill presents fine diagonal ribs and a comparatively smooth reverse.
  • GauzeA lightweight open fabric family provides a sheer, airy surface.
  • MatelasséA sculpted woven surface creates a quilted-looking pattern.
  • Oxford clothA basket-weave shirting groups warp yarns together to create visible texture.
  • PercaleA smooth plain-woven fabric is familiar as a crisp style of sheeting.
  • Pinpoint OxfordA finer Oxford variation retains grouped crossings with a smoother, smaller-scale texture.
  • PoplinA closely woven plain-weave fabric offers a smooth shirting surface, sometimes with a fine crosswise rib.
  • SateenA satin-structured fabric, commonly made with cotton yarns, presents a smooth face.
  • SeersuckerAlternating puckered and smoother bands give this cloth its recognizable relief.
  • VelvetAn extra warp system forms a dense pile above the woven ground.
  • Woven piquéRaised woven ribs or small geometric textures give piqué a patterned surface.
  • Woven terryUncut loops project from a woven ground to create a toweling surface.
