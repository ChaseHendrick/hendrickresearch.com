Fresco

Also searched as: tropical fresco, high-twist fresco, fresco cloth, fresco suiting, open-weave fresco

Fresco is a high-twist, relatively open suiting cloth, usually worsted wool, made for warm-weather wear with a dry, porous hand.

In detail

In mill and tailor language, fresco describes a cloth built from highly twisted worsted yarns set with enough openness that air can move through the fabric. The high twist keeps the yarn springy and helps the cloth resist wrinkling in heat, while an open sett leaves more space between yarns; the finished weight still depends on yarn size and construction. Hardy Minnis historically used Fresco as a trademark for such cloths; the same word is now often used generically for similar high-twist tropical suitings from other mills. Treat a hangtag's fresco claim as a construction hint, then confirm fiber, twist character, sett and weight.

The science and numbers

Yarn twist and fabric openness describe different structural levels. Twist binds fibers into yarn; sett describes how closely yarns are placed in the cloth. An open plain weave does not require the crossed warp construction of leno. Evaluate the weave diagram, yarn specification and finished fabric separately. Air permeability, crease recovery and abrasion claims need defined fabric tests, while a cloth name alone supplies none of those measurements. Similar-looking suitings can differ in mass, composition, finishing and lining, so avoid transferring a result between products.

A practical example

Compare two swatches of similar stated weight, one an open tropical suiting and the other a compact cloth. Inspect yarn spacing against a light, then compare the surface and recovery after gently folding each sample. This is an observation exercise, not an air-permeability or wearer-comfort test. A lining or close garment fit can change the experience again.

What to distinguish

Fresco is a cloth construction and trade language, not a fiber grade or a Super S number. A fresco label does not prove Australian merino, a particular micron or Hardy Minnis origin. Because Fresco began as a trademark and later entered loose trade use, ask whether the word is a brand name, a mill style name or only a salesperson's shorthand. Open, high-twist cloths can still pill, shine or bag if the finishing and garment design are poor.

Origins and history

Huddersfield Fine Worsteds describes its Fresco collection as continuing fabrics patented in 1907. This is an attributed producer history. Contemporary buyers also use fresco-style as a comparison for other open tropical suitings; that wording does not identify the original producer. Record the mill, collection and cloth reference when provenance matters, and distinguish the collection name from the generic wool content.
