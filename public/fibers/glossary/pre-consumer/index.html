Pre-consumer

Also searched as: post-industrial, manufacturing waste

Material diverted from a manufacturing waste stream, subject to the applicable definition.

In detail

Pre-consumer material is recovered from manufacturing before a product reaches its end user. Eligibility for a recycled-content claim depends on the applicable definition. Rework or scrap returned within its originating process may be treated differently from material diverted from a separate waste stream.

The science and numbers

Pre-consumer streams include cutting-room offcuts, yarn and spinning waste, and finishing waste, and each behaves differently. A 2026 study of rotor yarns found that droppings from spinning blended with cotton with limited quality loss, whereas brush fiber shed during sueding or emerizing of dyed fabric gave acceptable yarns with higher mass irregularity, imperfections and hairiness, because its fibers were shorter; the waste did, however, supply color without further dyeing. Because such streams are clean and well characterized, they also suit non-textile uses: one company's post-industrial cotton patches were shredded into fibers of at most 35 mm, pretreated with alkaline mercerization wastewater and enzymatically hydrolyzed, recovering about 90 percent of the glucose for fermentation to lactic acid. The authors cite estimates that, globally, less than 1 percent of textile waste is recycled back into new garments.

A practical example

For cutting-room offcuts, ask where they were generated, how they were collected and what recycling operation transformed them. The example describes a possible input stream; it does not certify the eligibility of every offcut under every scheme.

What to distinguish

The rules can change. Textile Exchange published 2026 updates concerning reclaimed-material tracking and an alternative definition for certain pre-consumer plastics. Check the effective standard and claim scope instead of treating an older general definition as a current certification decision.

Origins and history

The prefix marks when the waste arises relative to the consumer. Older US wool law blurred the line, since the 1939 Wool Products Labeling Act placed unused manufactured products and used goods in a single 'recycled wool' category. Voluntary standards later separated the two and debated which manufacturing scrap qualifies. Commercial textile-to-textile recycling often started with pre-consumer material: Lenzing states that its REFIBRA technology, introduced in 2017, first used cotton cutting waste before adding post-consumer textiles.
