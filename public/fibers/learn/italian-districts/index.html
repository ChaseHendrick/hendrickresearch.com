Italian textile districts: Biella, Prato, Como.

Mill towns are finishing cultures, not fiber origins.

Reviewed 2026-09-26. Open in the interactive atlas.

  2. Prato and regenerated wool
  3. Como and silk

Biella and worsted wool

Biella in Piedmont has a long textile history, with industrial wool processing developing strongly in the nineteenth century. A mill name can identify a processing business, but it does not by itself locate the sheep farm. Ask separately about fiber origin and the stages performed by the mill.

Prato and regenerated wool

Prato in Tuscany is associated with cardato, or woollen cloth, including regenerated wool made from recovered textiles. Cardato describes woollen preparation; it is not by itself a recycled-content percentage. Sorting, opening, blending and spinning are separate operations. Ask what the input was and how much recovered wool the finished cloth contains.

Como and silk

Como’s lakeside mills are known for silk weaving, printing and finishing, including cloth made from yarn that may have been reeled elsewhere. Silk fiber origin, throwster, weaver and printer can sit in different countries. Read each stage separately.

Mill name is not fiber origin

Italian districts concentrate know-how, capital equipment and trade networks. They often import fiber and export cloth. When a brand says Biella wool or Como silk, ask which processes happened there and where the fiber was grown or reeled. The atlas already marks Biella and Prato as hubs on illustrative routes; those markers remain educational models, not supplier directories.
