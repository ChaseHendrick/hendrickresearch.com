A label is a beginning. Follow the rest of the story.

Five questions that take you beyond a fiber percentage.

Reviewed 2026-09-22. Open in the interactive atlas.

On this page

  1. Start with composition
  2. Separate origin from manufacture
  3. Ask for evidence that matches the claim
  4. Look at the cloth in front of you
  5. Use the care label as a design clue

Start with composition

Read every component, including linings and any elastane. A fabric called a wool coat can include a blend, a synthetic lining and interfacing. Record the stated percentages before comparing products.

Separate origin from manufacture

The country where a garment was assembled may be different from the places where its fiber was grown, spun and woven. Ask for those stages separately. A mill name is useful only if you know what that mill actually did.

Ask for evidence that matches the claim

A farm-level claim, a chemical-testing claim and a recycled-content claim answer different questions. Ask whether the claim applies to the specific product, and whether a current certificate or transaction record supports it.

Look at the cloth in front of you

Handle, seams, weight, recovery and fit affect whether a garment will be worn for years. Fiber names can help frame questions; they cannot replace inspection of a finished piece.

Use the care label as a design clue

A material may sound easy to wash while its lining, dye or finish demands something different. The whole garment’s instructions should govern care.
